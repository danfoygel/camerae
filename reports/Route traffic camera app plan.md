# Stitch public DOT camera feeds onto driving routes

You can build this app today from free public data, and video is not the hard part. US state DOTs and their 511 vendors publish roughly **47,000 camera locations** across all 50 states ([Ora](https://github.com/warner-wvez/Ora)). Most of those states run on **three vendor platforms**: Arcadis/IBI 511, Castle Rock CARS and Iteris ATIS. One adapter per platform, plus about ten one-off state adapters, reaches nearly the whole national inventory. Researchers' direct probes on 2026-11-18 found about **9,000 HLS streams in ten states** (CA, CO, MN, IA, IN, MO, MD, DE, SC, AL) that need no key, carry no token and send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. Those streams play inline in a browser with hls.js and no server relay. Routing and geocoding are a commodity: Mapbox's free tier, a paid Stadia plan, or self-hosted Valhalla all work. Matching cameras to a route is a standard two-step GIS job: a corridor search, then linear referencing along the route line. The real constraints sit elsewhere. Licensing is mostly unwritten or restrictive, many feeds are undocumented website backends, a minority of streams use tokens or licenses (KS, GA, FL, NC, PA, CT), and some camera licenses explicitly bar use while driving. The recommended plan is a server-side camera catalog with a thin media resolver and a snapshot-first client that runs one live player at a time. It starts with the keyless HLS states, adds the keyed IBI 511 adapter second, and treats commercial aggregators (TrafficLand, INRIX) as a licensing decision rather than an engineering one.

## Three vendor platforms carry most of America's 47,000 cameras

The camera inventory is fragmented by state but concentrated by vendor. That concentration decides how much ingest code you write. **Arcadis/IBI 511** ("TravelIQ") runs AZ, NV, UT, ID, WI, GA, FL, PA, CT, LA and NC, plus Alaska, Ontario and New Brunswick. Every one of these sites exposes the same keyed endpoint, `/api/v2/get/cameras?key=&format=json`, which returns Id, Roadway, Direction, Latitude, Longitude, Location and `Views[]` with image and video URLs ([AZ511 GET Cameras](https://az511.com/help/endpoint/cameras); [511LA GET Cameras](https://511la.org/help/endpoint/cameras)). The terms are identical across these sites: a registered account, a developer key, and **"Ten calls every 60 seconds"** ([UDOT API doc](https://udottraffic.utah.gov/developers/doc); [511GA API docs](https://511ga.org/developers/doc)). A port of the MIT-licensed Home Assistant integration ha-the511 found that **21 of its 24 providers run on TravelIQ**, and the other three run on the Iteris/ATG GeoJSON CDN ([ha-the511](https://github.com/ihavespoken327/ha-the511)).

**Castle Rock CARS** runs CO, MN, IA, KS, IN and NE, and now also the 511NY front end. The CARS states return one shared schema from `https://{st}tg.carsprogram.org/cameras_v1/api/cameras`. Each record has lat/lon, routeId, a milepost-style `linearReference`, and views typed `STILL_IMAGE` or `WMP` (HLS). The endpoint was public JSON with `ACAO: *` and no key when probed ([MN CARS](https://mntg.carsprogram.org/cameras_v1/api/cameras); [CO CARS](https://cotg.carsprogram.org/cameras_v1/api/cameras)). New York moved off IBI. Its new site config points at `api-511x-nysdot.carsprogram.org/cameras`, and the old documented `getcameras` path now returns the new single-page app's HTML ([511NY CARS config](https://demo.511ny.org/configs/main.json?v=1.10.0)). **Iteris ATIS** runs South Carolina and Montana. South Carolina's keyless GeoJSON lists 801 cameras with route, mile marker, direction and stream URLs ([SC cameras GeoJSON](https://sc.cdn.iteris-atis.com/geojson/icons/metadata/icons.cameras.geojson)).

Several of the best feeds sit outside these platforms. Caltrans CWWP2 is the best-documented feed in the country. It publishes 12 district files in JSON, XML, CSV and TXT with **3,615 cameras, 2,313 of them with HLS**. Records include route, postmile, view direction and 12 historical JPEGs, and Caltrans states "There is no charge for the use of this data" ([CWWP2 CCTV](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/documentation/cctv/cctv.htm); [cctvStatusD07.json](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/data/d7/cctv/cctvStatusD07.json)). Maryland CHART publishes 553 cameras with milepost and untokenized HLS ([CHART camera JSON](https://chartexp1.sha.maryland.gov/CHARTExportClientService/getCameraMapDataJSON.do)). Delaware publishes 372 ([DelDOT JSON](https://tmc.deldot.gov/json/videocamera.json)), Missouri 875, all HLS ([MoDOT StreamingCams2.json](https://traveler.modot.org/timconfig/feed/desktop/StreamingCams2.json)), and Alabama's ALGO 652 with HLS and DASH ([ALGO v4 Cameras](https://api.algotraffic.com/v4/Cameras)).

### Aggregators trade money for licensing clarity, not for coverage

Commercial aggregators matter mainly because they come with redistribution rights. **TrafficLand** says it has contractual redistribution agreements with **more than 50 DOTs**, including 1,600+ TxDOT cameras. It delivers JPEG and **RTSP H.264 at 192 kbps and 12–15 fps**, not HLS, and pricing is by quote ([World Highways](https://www.worldhighways.com/wh12/news/trafficland-agreement-texas-dot-access-traffic-cameras); [FirstNet listing](https://developer.att.com/firstnet/apis-sdks/trafficland)). **INRIX**, with imagery from Vizzion, offers `GetTrafficCamerasInBox`/`InRadius` queries and a `GetTrafficCameraVideoURL` that answers with a 302 redirect. Each camera carries license flags: **`useWhileDrivingProhibited`, `feeToEndUserProhibited` and `sublicenseProhibited`**, plus a mandatory copyright notice ([INRIX docs](https://docs.inrix.com/traffic/trafficcameras/)). **Road511** is a cheap unified 511 API at $29–$279 a month. It claims 64 US and Canadian jurisdictions and returns camera features with image URLs, but no video and no redistribution license ([Road511](https://road511.com/)). It works as a metadata bootstrap or a cross-check, not as a media source. **Windy Webcams** is mostly scenic webcams with stills and timelapses, requires a link back to Windy, and its pro tier costs €9,990 a year ([Windy pricing](https://api.windy.com/webcams/pricing); [Windy terms](https://api.windy.com/webcams/terms)). It is a poor fit. No camera imagery offering was found from HERE or TomTom.

### Open-source projects are blueprints, not foundations

The closest existing project is **Ora**. It maps 46,853 cameras in all 50 states as per-state GeoJSON, plays HLS in 22 states, and relays Florida and New Jersey because those hosts "lock out other origins". Its sweep on 2026-10-22 found **7,637 cameras down** ([Ora](https://github.com/warner-wvez/Ora)). Ora uses the **PolyForm Noncommercial** license, so treat it as a map of what is possible and re-derive the data yourself. **ha-the511** (MIT) can be ported directly for the IBI and Iteris endpoint shapes ([ha-the511](https://github.com/ihavespoken327/ha-the511)). OpenTrafficCamMap documents odd formats such as TxDOT's POST-and-Base64 image service ([OpenTrafficCamMap](https://github.com/AidanWelch/OpenTrafficCamMap)). **OpenStreetMap** can tag traffic cameras (`man_made=surveillance` + `surveillance:zone=traffic`) but stores no feed URLs ([OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:surveillance:zone)). It adds little, and merging it into your catalog raises ODbL share-alike questions.

Outside the US, Canada is the easiest expansion. Ontario and New Brunswick run the same IBI API ([511ON](https://511on.ca/developers/doc)), and DriveBC webcams are licensed for commercial use under OGL-BC ([BC gov](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2016TRAN0045-000360)). London's TfL JamCams offer only stills and about 9-second MP4 clips ([TfL Techforum](https://techforum.tfl.gov.uk/t/live-jamcam-images-and-clips/596)), and England's National Highways has no official camera API ([petition](https://repetition.me/657108-enable-api-access-to-national-highways-traffic-camera-cctv-images)).

## About 9,000 streams play in a browser today; the rest need a server

HLS from Wowza-style `/rtplive/.../playlist.m3u8` origins is now the dominant public video format. The researchers found no public MJPEG, RTSP or DASH, with two exceptions: Alabama also offers DASH, and South Carolina's Skyline/skyvdn records list RTSP and RTMP alongside HTTPS HLS ([SC GeoJSON](https://sc.cdn.iteris-atis.com/geojson/icons/metadata/icons.cameras.geojson)). The verified open hosts include Caltrans `wzmedia.dot.ca.gov` (1280x720 H.264), Minnesota, Iowa, Indiana, Missouri (320x240), Colorado, Maryland, Delaware, South Carolina (720x480 at about 310 kbps), Alabama's Wowza CDN, Louisiana, Wisconsin and Nevada. All of them sent `ACAO: *` over HTTPS ([Caltrans sample](https://wzmedia.dot.ca.gov/D7/CCTV-196.stream/playlist.m3u8); [MD sample](https://strmr5.sha.maryland.gov/rtplive/7a00a1dc01250075004d823633235daa/playlist.m3u8); [SC sample](https://s18.us-east-1.skyvdn.com/rtplive/50001/playlist.m3u8)). One source conflicts with this. OpenTrafficCamMap calls Colorado streaming DRM-protected ([OpenTrafficCamMap](https://github.com/AidanWelch/OpenTrafficCamMap)), but the 2026-11-18 probe played 815 Colorado HLS views keylessly, and the newer direct test should win. Whether Skyline-hosted video is open varies by state. It is open in SC, returns **401** in GA ([GA sample](https://sfs-msc-pub-lq-01.navigator.dot.ga.gov/rtplive/BART-CCTV-0209/playlist.m3u8)), and has already been cut off from at least one third party in TN after heavy downloading ([Knox News via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/private-companies-clash-over-tdot-090551143.html)).

The failure modes are known and each has a specific fix. **Tokens**: Kansas JWTs last five minutes. Pennsylvania's HLS has used expiring Wowza `wmsAuthSign` tokens behind an AWS WAF that admits only "authorized URL links and IP addresses" ([CloudHesive](https://www.cloudhesive.com/case-study/goverment/commonwealth-of-pennsylvania-helps-travelers-get-to-destinations-faster-by-securely-delivering-traffic-video-streams-online/)). **Auth-required IBI video** (GA, FL, NC, CT) is flagged `isVideoAuthRequired: true` ([511GA list](https://511ga.org/List/GetData/Cameras); [FL511 list](https://fl511.com/List/GetData/Cameras)). Reproducing the website's internal token call would most likely breach site terms, so use snapshots there or negotiate access. **Broken CORS**: IBI image endpoints send duplicate or comma-joined `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` headers ([AZ511 image](https://az511.com/map/Cctv/682)). That is harmless in an `<img>` tag but fatal to `fetch()` or canvas use. **Mixed content and RTMP**: New Mexico serves snapshots over HTTP only and video over RTMP only ([NMRoads](https://servicev5.nmroads.com/RealMapWAR/GetCameraInfo)). **URL churn**: Iowa changed every image and video URL on 2026-05-20 without notice and now tells integrators to pull current URLs from its open-data feed ([Iowa DOT advisory](https://iowadot.gov/news/2026-05-28/media-advisory-iowa-dot-updates-traffic-camera-video-system-data-feed-usersdevelopers)). NCDOT retired its keyless API on 2026-05-27 ([NCDOT legacy endpoint](https://eapps.ncdot.gov/services/traffic-prod/v1/cameras)).

Native apps escape part of this. CORS applies only to browsers, so iOS and Android clients can hit most DOT origins directly. They still need App Transport Security or cleartext-traffic exceptions for HTTP-only hosts. This is one of the strongest technical arguments for a native client. A browser client needs a small proxy for the long tail.

### Decoder limits make "one live player" a hard rule

On the web, use **hls.js 1.7.x** (v1.7.4, 28 Oct 2026). It runs on Media Source Extensions and on Apple's ManagedMediaSource, so it also works in iPhone Safari 17.1+ ([hls.js releases](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js/releases); [hls.js README](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js/)). Prefer native `video.src` playback on Safari for power efficiency. Skip Video.js, whose version 10 was still in beta through August 2026 ([Video.js changelog](https://videojs.org/changelog/10.0.0-beta.27.md)). On iOS, use AVPlayer. On Android, use Media3 ExoPlayer, whose `DefaultPreloadManager` is built to preload the next item in a feed ([Media3 1.8.0](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/08/media3-180-whats-new.html?hl=en)). The binding limit is hardware decoders, not element count. In WebKit a **paused video still holds a hardware decoder** ([WebKit 193449](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=193449)), and developers report AVPlayer failing past a device-dependent number of active players, in one case at the 19th video in a feed ([Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/67382)). A route list with 40 cameras therefore needs snapshot rows plus a single shared live player, attached on tap or to the most-visible row and fully released on exit. That design is required, not a style choice.

For the rare camera that offers only RTSP, RTMP or MJPEG, **go2rtc** or **MediaMTX** can ingest it on demand and re-serve it as HLS, WebRTC or JPEG snapshots, transcoding only when needed ([go2rtc](https://github.com/AlexxIT/go2rtc); [MediaMTX review](https://unsubbed.pages.dev/tools/mediamtx/)). Run these per tap, never fleet-wide. Plain HLS latency of 10–30 seconds is fine for checking traffic; Caltrans District 7 reports cutting latency "by up to 29 seconds" after moving off HLS ([Red5](https://www.red5.net/?p=67725)). WebRTC is therefore unnecessary.

## Routing is a commodity, and Valhalla doubles as the matching engine

Any mainstream router can produce standard directions. What separates the options is cost, terms and whether the geometry suits camera matching. **Google** is the strongest product, but the $200 monthly credit ended in March 2025. Free usage is now capped per SKU, for example 10,000 Compute Routes Essentials per month and then $5 per 1,000 ([Google pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). Its terms also bar using Directions and Geocoding content **"with a non-Google map"** ([Google Service Specific Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)), which locks you into the Google map SDK. **Mapbox** offers **100,000 Directions** and **100,000 temporary geocodes** free per month, plus 50,000 web map loads. Its autocomplete (Search Box) includes only 2,500 free sessions at standard pricing, and temporary geocodes cannot be stored ([Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing)). **Apple MapKit JS** includes **250,000 map views and 25,000 service calls per day** per developer membership ([Apple MapKit JS](https://developer.apple.com/maps/mapkitjs/)), which is compelling for an Apple-first product. **Stadia Maps** is hosted Valhalla with geocoding and OSM tiles. Its free plan is non-commercial, and paid plans start at $20 a month for 1M credits ([Stadia pricing](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/)).

| Option | Free allowance | Strength | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mapbox Directions + Geocoding | 100k routes, 100k temporary geocodes per month ([Mapbox](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing)) | Best metered free tier; works with MapLibre-style maps | Autocomplete sessions run out first (2.5k); no storing temporary geocodes |
| Google Routes + Places | 10k Essentials per SKU per month ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)) | Best address and POI quality | Must use a Google map; more expensive per call |

| Stadia (hosted Valhalla) | 200k credits per month, non-commercial ([Stadia](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/)) | Open stack, cheap paid tier | Free plan excludes commercial use |
| Self-hosted Valhalla + Photon | Unlimited | Same engine routes and map-matches cameras | You run a 32–64 GB server and rebuild monthly |

Self-hosting favors **Valhalla**. Its route shape comes as polyline6, each maneuver carries `street_names`, `bearing_before`/`bearing_after`, shape indices and time, and its `trace_attributes` map matcher returns OSM `way_id`s and headings for each edge ([Valhalla route API](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/route/api-reference/); [Valhalla map-matching API](https://valhalla.github.io/valhalla/api/map-matching/api-reference)). One engine therefore computes the route and also snaps every camera to an OSM way at ingest time. **OSRM** is faster, but building a North America extract needs roughly 64–128 GB of RAM ([OSM help](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/49651/hardware-requirements-drivetime-server)). A full planet build of Nominatim needs 64 GB of RAM and about 900 GB of SSD ([Nominatim import docs](https://nominatim.org/release-docs/develop/admin/Import/)), and Nominatim is weak at type-ahead, so use Photon for autocomplete. For the base map, MapLibre with **OpenFreeMap** costs nothing and needs no key ([OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org)). Self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles is the fallback if that community service changes.

## Linear referencing turns a corridor search into an ordered camera list

Matching is the app's core algorithm, and it is simple. Precompute at ingest, then do two cheap steps per route. At ingest, normalize each camera's roadway to a canonical reference (so "Interstate 80", "I 80 W" and "I-80" all become "I-80"). Convert its view direction to a bearing. Optionally snap it to an OSM way with Valhalla, since IBI records carry no milepost, while MD, SC, KY, AL and CARS records do. At query time, decode the route polyline. Find **candidates** by testing a corridor about 50–150 m wide against a static spatial index: flatbush or kdbush in JS, an STRtree in Shapely, or `ST_DWithin` on a PostGIS GiST index. Then **project** each candidate onto the line for its distance along the route. PostGIS `ST_LineLocatePoint` returns that position as a 0–1 fraction ([PostGIS docs](https://postgis.net/docs/ST_LineLocatePoint.html)), and Turf's `nearestPointOnLine` returns `totalDistance`, `pointDistance` and `segmentIndex` ([Turf README](https://github.com/Turfjs/turf/blob/master/packages/turf-nearest-point-on-line/README.md)). Sorting by that distance gives the camera order.

Filtering decides how good the list feels. Reject candidates more than about 30–60 m off the line. Compare the camera's view or road bearing against the route bearing at the projected point, computed from the two polyline vertices on either side rather than from a provider's turn-step bearing. A difference near 90° indicates an overpass or cross street. Match the normalized road reference against the maneuver's street names. The strongest test is way membership: is the camera's precomputed `way_id` in the route's set of ways from Valhalla `trace_attributes`? This matters because a plain buffer "ignores network topology" ([2GIS Turf docs](https://docs.2gis.com/en/mapgl/turfjs)) and will happily match a frontage-road camera. Collapse cameras within about 50–100 m into one site with several views. For out-and-back or cloverleaf routes, project within a window around the segment the candidate came from, not onto the whole line. Get ETA by interpolating cumulative maneuver or segment durations at each camera's distance. These thresholds are engineering defaults rather than benchmarked values, so tune them against real routes. The arithmetic is light: about 50,000 points against a route of 5,000–20,000 vertices should take milliseconds in memory. It can run server-side in PostGIS or client-side over a few-megabyte GeoJSON file. Server-side is better when catalogs refresh often or when provider terms restrict where route geometry can go.

## Licensing, not technology, is the binding constraint

Few DOTs publish an explicit license for camera imagery, and the ones that do point in different directions. Caltrans treats its web content as public domain "unless otherwise indicated", but warns that it uses copyrighted photographs that "may require additional permissions" ([Caltrans Conditions of Use](https://dot.ca.gov/conditions-of-use)). BC licenses its webcams for commercial reuse ([BC gov](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2016TRAN0045-000360)). At the other extreme, Arkansas **prohibits embedding or even linking** its camera images from another app ([IDrive Arkansas terms](https://site.idrivearkansas.com/index.php/policies/camera-terms-of-use)). CTroads limits use to "individuals seeking consumer traveler info" and bans reverse engineering, on pain of a permanent IP block ([CTroads Terms](https://ctroads.org/termsandconditions)). PennDOT requires source attribution and a separate video license ([PennDOT](https://www.pa.gov/services/penndot/request-access-to-transportation-related-data-feeds)), and MassDOT, 511NY and Wisconsin all use developer agreements. One developer pulled TxDOT and Houston TranStar imagery from a public site until a data-use agreement was signed ([cruzewebsite PR](https://github.com/cruzemaps/cruzewebsite/pull/111)). Most of the keyless endpoints that make the MVP easy are undocumented website backends with no published terms: CARS `cameras_v1`, MoDOT, TxDOT ITS, OKTraffic, the SC Iteris CDN, ALGO, and IBI `/List/GetData/Cameras`. Silence is a gap, not permission.

A second legal issue is specific to this product's main use case. INRIX's `useWhileDrivingProhibited` flag shows that at least some camera licenses passed through aggregators forbid exactly the in-car viewing this app invites ([INRIX docs](https://docs.inrix.com/traffic/trafficcameras/)). Whatever the license says, a scrolling list of live video is a distraction risk for a driver. The defensible design is trip planning before departure, passenger use, or use while parked, with a snapshot-first UI. CarPlay and Android Auto video policies were not researched and need checking before any in-car integration. Two dependencies carry their own terms. Google requires a Google map for its routing content, and Ora's noncommercial license rules out copying its data compilation into a commercial product.

## Conclusion: build the catalog first, then add states by vendor

The research changes the problem's center of gravity. Free inline video is already available for about a fifth of the national inventory and snapshots for most of the rest. The hard parts are keeping URLs current across 50 changing feeds, and having the standing to show the imagery. The winning asset is therefore a **normalized, health-checked, license-tagged camera catalog**, not a player or a router. Building it per vendor family rather than per state keeps engineering work roughly flat as coverage grows. Commercial aggregators are best understood as a way to buy legal clarity for tier C–E states and for a paid product, rather than as a coverage shortcut.

### Recommended architecture

The architecture has five components. They are deliberately decoupled so that a DOT changing its feed breaks one adapter and not the app.

| Component | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Ingest and camera catalog (backend)** | Scheduled workers, one adapter per vendor family (CARS, IBI 511, Iteris GeoJSON) plus bespoke adapters (Caltrans CWWP2, MD CHART, DelDOT, MoDOT, ALGO, then WA, OR, KY, TX and others). Poll politely, use `ETag`/`Last-Modified`, and pull IBI states in bulk every few minutes to stay under 10 calls per minute. Normalize into one schema in PostGIS: stable ID, agency, lat/lon, normalized road reference, direction or bearing, milepost if present, and a list of media descriptors typed `hls`, `hls_tokenized`, `jpeg_refresh`, `mp4_clip`, `rtmp/rtsp` or `custom`. Add status and health (last successful fetch, stream liveness), attribution text and logo, and per-source license flags (embedding allowed, video allowed, commercial use, use while driving). Precompute OSM `way_id` and road bearing with Valhalla at ingest. |
| **Routing and geocoding** | MVP: hosted Mapbox Directions plus Mapbox geocoding or a cheaper autocomplete provider (or Stadia's paid plan), rendered on MapLibre with OpenFreeMap tiles. Growth: self-hosted Valhalla plus Photon on one 32–64 GB box, rebuilt monthly from a Geofabrik US extract, so routing, map matching and camera snapping share one engine. Use Google only if you accept the Google map SDK. Use native MapKit if the app goes iOS-only. |
| **Camera-to-route matching** | A server endpoint that takes the route polyline and returns the ordered camera list. Steps: corridor candidate search (about 100 m) on the spatial index, projection for distance along the route, filters on perpendicular distance, bearing, road reference and `way_id` membership, de-duplication into sites, and ETA interpolation. Return distance, ETA, direction-of-travel label and a media descriptor ID for each site. |
| **Media resolver and proxy** | A thin service, in order of preference. (1) Return the DOT's direct HLS URL whenever it is open and CORS-clean, for zero video bandwidth cost. (2) Mint fresh short-lived URLs at view time (Kansas JWTs; anything tokenized that is legitimately public). (3) Run a snapshot cache that polls each viewed camera's JPEG about every 30–60 s and serves it from a CDN with a short `max-age`, so the DOT sees one request per camera rather than one per user; this also fixes broken CORS and HTTP-only hosts. (4) Rewrite playlists only where CORS or HTTPS is broken. (5) Start an on-demand go2rtc/MediaMTX relay for RTMP- or RTSP-only cameras, torn down when idle. Never relay video a DOT has locked to its own site without written permission. |
| **Client** | A route list in route order. Each row shows a snapshot refreshed only while visible, the camera name, direction, distance/ETA and a LIVE badge. One shared live player attaches on tap or to the most-visible row, with the snapshot as poster, and the next camera along the route is preloaded. Use hls.js (with native HLS on Safari) on the web, AVPlayer on iOS, and Media3 on Android. If live playback fails, fall back silently to the snapshot. Show DOT attribution on every tile. Present the app as pre-trip/passenger/parked, with a glanceable snapshot mode as the default in motion. |

### Key risks

| Risk | Evidence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Licensing and terms per state** | Arkansas bans embedding and linking; CT limits use to individuals; PA and TX require agreements ([IDrive Arkansas](https://site.idrivearkansas.com/index.php/policies/camera-terms-of-use); [CTroads](https://ctroads.org/termsandconditions)) | A per-source license matrix in the catalog; written permission before launch; attribution on every tile; honor agency on/off flags |
| **Undocumented endpoints** | CARS, MoDOT, TxDOT, OKTraffic, the Iteris CDN, ALGO and IBI `/List/GetData` are website backends; reCAPTCHA already loads on the IA, IN and KS sites | Prefer documented keyed APIs where they exist; cache server-side; alert on adapter failures; keep snapshot fallbacks |
| **Tokenized and locked streams** | KS 300 s JWTs; GA and FL 401; PA WAF and tokens; Ora relays FL and NJ ([CloudHesive](https://www.cloudhesive.com/case-study/goverment/commonwealth-of-pennsylvania-helps-travelers-get-to-destinations-faster-by-securely-delivering-traffic-video-streams-online/); [Ora](https://github.com/warner-wvez/Ora)) | Resolve media at view time; never cache playable URLs long-term; do not defeat auth or Referer locks without agreement |
| **Feed churn and host cut-offs** | Iowa's unannounced URL change; NCDOT API retirement; TN host blocking a third party ([Iowa DOT](https://iowadot.gov/news/2026-05-28/media-advisory-iowa-dot-updates-traffic-camera-video-system-data-feed-usersdevelopers); [Knox News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/private-companies-clash-over-tdot-090551143.html)) | Health checks on every adapter; join DOT developer notification lists; polite polling; stream only while a user is watching |
| **Use while driving** | INRIX `useWhileDrivingProhibited` flag ([INRIX](https://docs.inrix.com/traffic/trafficcameras/)) | Pre-trip/passenger/parked framing; snapshot-only while moving; check CarPlay and Android Auto rules before any car integration |
| **Data quality and outages** | Caltrans records with the wrong county and stale `recordDate`; 7,637 cameras down nationally at Ora's last sweep | Trust geometry over metadata; hide cameras that fail a recent liveness check |
| **Provider terms** | Google requires a Google map; Mapbox temporary geocodes cannot be stored; Stadia's free plan is non-commercial; Ora is noncommercial; OSM is ODbL | Choose the routing stack with the client platform in mind; re-derive data rather than copying Ora; keep any OSM layer separate |

### Open decisions for you

| Decision | Options and trade-off |
|---|---|
| **Platform** | Web (MapLibre + hls.js) is fastest to ship, but needs the proxy for the CORS and HTTPS long tail. Native iOS (MapKit + AVPlayer) avoids CORS, makes routing nearly free, and fits Apple's media stack, but it is Apple-only. Cross-platform (React Native with react-native-video, or Flutter) covers both but needs custom MJPEG and player pooling work. |
| **Hosted vs. self-hosted routing** | Hosted Mapbox or Stadia for the MVP. Self-hosted Valhalla + Photon once volume or way-ID matching justifies running a 32–64 GB server. |
| **Geographic scope** | The 10–11 Tier A states only (video-rich, legally simplest to ask about), versus national snapshots via the IBI and bespoke adapters, versus US + Canada. |
| **Commercial licensing** | Stay free and attribution-based with per-DOT permissions, or license TrafficLand or INRIX (quote-based; RTSP needs a relay; per-camera flags may forbid fees or in-car use) for clean redistribution rights and tier C–E video. |
| **Monetization** | Charging users interacts with INRIX `feeToEndUserProhibited`, with Stadia's and Ora's noncommercial terms, and with DOT goodwill. Decide this before Phase 0 outreach, because it changes what you ask each DOT. |
| **Video ambition** | Video-first (higher bandwidth, more legal exposure) versus snapshot-first with tap-to-play (cheaper, safer, works in every state). The evidence favors snapshot-first. |
